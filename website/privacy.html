Privacy, on your devices.

This page describes RIFT 0.9.0 and how its iPhone-to-Mac transfer functions are designed. Last updated November 15, 2026.

In short: RIFT pairs nearby devices and sends selected data directly over encrypted Apple MultipeerConnectivity sessions. The project does not provide a RIFT cloud account or run a RIFT file-upload server.

What RIFT accesses

On iPhone, RIFT requests access to the current Apple Music track and position for optional music handoff, motion sensors while its gesture mode is enabled and the app is active, and local network access for device discovery. When you choose an item through the Photos or Files picker, RIFT reads that selected photo, video or file for sending. It does not require blanket access to the Photos library.

RIFT reads clipboard text only after you select Send copied text. Link text is transferred only when you send a link. On Mac, the app receives those items and may request Automation permission to control the Music app for song handoff.

Where the data goes

Photos, videos and files selected for transfer travel to the Mac over a nearby encrypted MultipeerConnectivity session and are stored in your Mac's Downloads/RIFT folder. Links are displayed in the Mac app and are not opened automatically. Explicitly sent clipboard text is copied to the Mac clipboard.

The Mac name and approved peer identity are remembered locally to support reconnection. RIFT also saves your chosen animation effect and gesture sensitivity on your device. Those preferences do not require a RIFT account.

Third-party services

RIFT is hosted as open-source code and downloadable release files on GitHub. Its public website may request GitHub's release metadata to display the latest download buttons. GitHub may process ordinary website and download request information according to its own privacy policies. Clicking a creator profile or other external link takes you to that separate service.

Controls and limits

You choose which items to send. You can turn off motion-based music handoff, reset device pairing, or stop using RIFT. iOS may pause discovery or transfer when the app is in the background. The Mac receiver may remain active in the menu bar after you close its window.

RIFT is an independent project, not affiliated with Apple. Review the source code or visit Support for installation and permission questions.
